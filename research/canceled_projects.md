## 1. What was invalidated and what the Board declined to defend

### 1.1 The court ruling (merits)

On **March 31, 2026**, a three-judge panel of the **Virginia Court of Appeals** (Judges Stuart A. Raphael, Randolph A. Beales, and David Bernhard) unanimously **affirmed** the Prince William County Circuit Court and declared **void ab initio** the three zoning ordinances by which the Board of County Supervisors on December 13, 2023 created the PW Digital Gateway data-center corridor along Pageland Lane [valw2026; nbc42026; insidenova2026; virginiamercury2026].

The three voided ordinances and the rezoning applications they enacted are:

| Ordinance | Rezoning case | Project name | Developer of record |
|---|---|---|---|
| **23-57** | REZ2022-00032 | Digital Gateway **North** | QTS / GW Acquisition Co. |
| **23-58** | REZ2022-00033 | Digital Gateway **South** | QTS / GW Acquisition Co. |
| **23-59** | REZ2022-00036 | Compass PWC Campus 1 (f/k/a H&H Capital) | Compass Datacenters / H&H Capital Acquisitions |

Sources: PWC Planning Office, "PW Digital Gateway" [pwcva_dg]; potomaclocal2026a; fauquiernow2026.

The **Circuit Court decision** was issued by **Judge Kimberly A. Irving** on **August 7, 2025** in consolidated proceedings brought by the Oak Valley Homeowners' Association and twelve Oak Valley residents and by plaintiffs led by Katy Burke [wtop2025; valw2026; insidenova2026]. A parallel case before Circuit Judge James P. Hudson addressed post-complaint filings [valw2026].

The legal basis was procedural: the notices the county caused to be published in the *Washington Post* ahead of the Dec. 12–13, 2023 public hearing did not meet **Va. Code § 15.2-2204(A) and (B)** or the county's own **Zoning Ordinance § 32-700.60**. Specifically (per the Court of Appeals' opinion, as summarized by Virginia Lawyers Weekly):

- The advertisements did not appear on the dates the county had requested.
- Draft ordinances for the Compass application were **not publicly available** until **December 7, 2023**, even though ads ran Dec. 2–5.
- The two required publications failed the "successive weeks" and "at least six days between" requirements.

County Attorney Michelle Robl had advised the board to re-advertise; the applicants "agreed to assume the risk"; the board proceeded on a 4-3 vote after a 27-hour meeting [foxesellfaster2026; virginiamercury2026].

The Court of Appeals published the decision in **Oak Valley Homeowners Association, Inc. v. Board of County Supervisors of Prince William County**, Record Nos. **1584-25-4 / 1590-25-4 / 1592-25-4** (and a cross-caption docketed 2025-24-4), decided Mar. 31, 2026 [valw2026; caselawfindlaw2026]. The public circuit-court docket number was not captured in any of the secondary sources we reviewed; the underlying case is *Oak Valley Homeowners Ass'n, Inc., et al. v. Prince William Cnty. Bd. of Cnty. Supervisors, et al.*, Prince William Cnty. Cir. Ct. (decided Aug. 7, 2025). **Data gap: exact circuit-court case/style was not surfaced in public news coverage or in the published Court of Appeals caption retrieved via Justia and Findlaw; retrieval of the Prince William Circuit Court docket through the Officer of Court Records (eservice.pwcgov.org) is deferred to a later stage or in-person request.**

### 1.2 The non-appeal decision (April 15, 2026)

On **April 15, 2026**, the Prince William County Board of County Supervisors voted **unanimously** (all eight members present) to direct the County Attorney **not to seek further appellate review** in the Supreme Court of Virginia [potomaclocal2026a; wtop2026; washtimes2026; bisnow2026]. The board had previously authorized a cumulative **$1.72 million** in outside counsel fees to defend the rezonings [washtimes2026; bisnow2026]. The developers (QTS and Compass) retain an independent right to petition the Supreme Court of Virginia, because they were separately represented parties; as of the writing of this memo they have not indicated whether they will do so [wtop2026].

The effect of the non-appeal is that the Court of Appeals' mandate will stand: **the rezoning is void from inception**. For the parcels covered by REZ2022-00032, -00033, and -00036, the pre-December 2023 zoning classifications — **A-1 (Agricultural)** and **SR-5 (Semi-rural residential)** — are restored. The Comprehensive Plan Amendment **CPA2021-00004** (Res. No. 22-508, Nov. 1, 2022), which changed the long-range land use designation of the 2,139-acre corridor from AE/ER to Technology/Flex (T-3 Transect), **remains in effect** because it was not the subject of the litigation; but without a valid rezoning, no as-of-right data-center use is permitted on those parcels [pwcva_dg].

### 1.3 Board composition context

Then-Chair Ann Wheeler (D-At-Large) and three other Democrats produced the 4-3 December 2023 approval. Chair Deshundra Jefferson (D-At-Large), who defeated Wheeler in the June 2023 Democratic primary and took office January 2024, and Supervisors Bob Weir (R-Gainesville, the district in which Pageland Lane sits), Tom Gordy (R-Brentsville), and Yesli Vega (R-Coles) have publicly opposed the project [washtimes2026; nbc42026]. A re-advertised vote on the rezoning would almost certainly fail under the current board.

## 2. Parcel-by-parcel inventory (what was canceled, physically)

### 2.1 Corridor totals (CPA2021-00004 scope)

- **Total corridor area:** 2,139 acres across **194 parcels** south of Sudley Road, north of Route 29, between Pageland Lane and Route 234 [pwcva_dg].
- **Planned buildout (aggregate for the three voided rezonings):** approximately **23 million gross square feet** of data center space in up to **34–37 buildings** [datacenterfrontier2023; insidenova2026; bisnow2026; fauquiernow2026].
- **Target IT / utility capacity (aggregate):** Compass' updated application cited **up to 1.7 GW** at full buildout for its 884-acre tract [dcd2023compass; datacenterfrontier2023]. QTS' aggregate capacity was characterized as **~60 % of Compass's** (~1.0 GW) across its northern + southern tracts [datacenterfrontier2023]. We therefore carry **~2.7 GW of nameplate utility load** as the applicants' stated ambition. **Inferred note:** IT-load MW (the industry-standard "critical load" the county assesses under Va. Code § 58.1-3295.3) is typically 60–70 % of utility load; **we use 2.0 GW of IT load as the modelable build-out baseline** (load factor 0.74). See §4 for the tax-yield calculation.
- **Acreage by rezoning:**
  - REZ2022-00032 Digital Gateway North ≈ **534 acres** (QTS) [bisnow2023; multiple].
  - REZ2022-00033 Digital Gateway South ≈ **342 acres** (QTS).
  - REZ2022-00036 Compass PWC Campus 1 ≈ **884 acres** (Compass).
  - Remaining ≈ **379 acres** within the corridor were not covered by the three rezonings and had not yet been bundled into an active rezoning application as of April 2026 (see §3).

## 3. Other sites whose status is now contingent

The Oak Valley ruling did not directly invalidate any other rezoning. But its **signal** — that PWC's 2023 supervisors approved a data-center rezoning in violation of state law, and that a successor board will not defend the approval to the Supreme Court of Virginia — is the material fact for capital-at-risk analysis at every other site whose CAPEX depends on political stability of PWC overlay zoning. We classify contingent sites into three tiers.

### 3.1 Tier 1 — Direct procedural exposure

Any rezoning approved by the PWC Board between **November 2022** (when CPA2021-00004 was adopted) and **December 2023** (when CPA2021-00004's rezonings were voted) under the **same advertising / ordinance-availability practice** that the Court of Appeals found defective. As of this writing we have not identified another PWC data-center rezoning approved in that window with a surviving plaintiff pool, but a docket-level review of PWC Planning Office rezoning log for 2023 is needed. **Data gap.**

### 3.2 Tier 2 — Pending applications within the overlay whose political cover has evaporated

- **John Marshall Commons Tech Park (CTP-II LLC)** — 22.72 acres at Catharpin Road and John Marshall Highway, proposing two buildings totaling **up to 80 MW**. Planning Commission tied 4-4 in early 2026; the application remains live but has not advanced [dcd2026ctp].
- **Dulles South Innovation Center** — 1,930 acres along Sanders Lane (immediately north of Pageland Lane), assembled by LSI 360 across more than 250 parcels. As of **December 17, 2025** the applicant had filed only a cultural-resource-assessment pre-application; no rezoning case has been filed [virginiabusiness2025; pwtimes2025dulles; protectpwc2025]. On a gross-acreage basis this project is ~90 % the scale of the Digital Gateway. Given the Oak Valley precedent and the identity of its lobbying firm with the Digital Gateway team, full application filing now carries materially higher execution risk. **We score this contingent.**
- **Sanders Lane supplementary parcels** — pre-application activity described in the same Bristow Beat / Fauquier Now coverage. Contingent.

### 3.3 Tier 3 — Data Center Opportunity Zone Overlay District (DCOZOD) comprehensive review

- **DPA2021-00020** — the comprehensive review of the DCOZOD initiated by Res. 21-327 on May 18, 2021, has not yet been set for a Planning Commission or Board hearing [pwcva_dcozod]. The fact that a court has now told PWC that a headline overlay-based rezoning was procedurally void — and that the Board will not defend it — affects the signaling value of *every* overlay change still in the review pipeline. Data-center developers will reasonably demand **larger confidence discounts** when siting new CAPEX inside DCOZOD until the county demonstrates (a) procedural rigor and (b) political durability.

### 3.4 Tier 4 — Overlay rezonings that have been upheld

Not contingent on Oak Valley, for symmetry and to avoid overstating the impact:

- **Devlin Technology Park** — 269-acre Bristow tech park approved in November 2023 (separate applicant: Stanley Martin Homes / land; Amazon AWS acquired 189 acres for **$700 million** in November 2025). A Circuit Court challenge was dismissed in 2024 and the **Court of Appeals affirmed on September 16, 2025** — the **opposite result** from Oak Valley, on different facts [insidenova2025devlin; virginiabusiness2025amazon]. **Status: proceeding.**
- **Hornbaker Road** — 40-acre rezoning approved by 4-3 vote on **March 4, 2026**, outside the overlay district, with a special-use permit. Applicant: MJV Parcel A LLC / PWC-Parcel B LLC (Mortenson Development, contract purchaser) [insidenova2026hornbaker]. **Status: proceeding.**
- **Amazon Wellington/Devlin 44-acre acquisition** — $120 million land-only purchase closed February 2024 [dcd2024amazon44]. **Status: proceeding.**

## 4. Quantitative estimate of foregone tax yield at stabilization

### 4.1 Methodology and assumptions (all inputs labeled)

We estimate the annualized county tax yield that the three invalidated rezonings would have produced when fully built and depreciated to a mid-cycle steady state. This is the figure relevant to Pre-Cancellation Digital Gateway in the PWC fiscal model (Stage 6). The estimate integrates three revenue streams.

**Physical inputs (from §2):**
- Aggregate IT load at build-out: **2,000 MW** (2.0 GW). *Assumption: 74 % load factor applied to the developers' stated ~2.7 GW utility capacity; this is the conservative end of the industry range. Source of assumption: ProtectPWC and JLARC description of "up to 23 million sqft" with Compass at ~1.7 GW utility and QTS at ~1.0 GW utility.*
- Aggregate gross building area: **23,000,000 sqft** [datacenterfrontier2023; bisnow2026; foxesellfaster2026].

**County-specific unit economics (from TY 2024 PWC Data Center Revenue Report) [pwc_dc2024]:**
- TY 2024 Turnkey inventory: 29 buildings, 5,844,023 sqft, **862 MW** actual-and-imputed load, total real-property assessed value **$8.1788 billion**.
  - Derived **real-property assessed value per MW at stabilization:** $8.1788B / 862 MW = **$9.49 million / MW**.
  - Derived **real-property assessed value per sqft:** $8.1788B / 5,844,023 sqft = **$1,399 / sqft**.
- TY 2024 Computer-equipment & peripherals (C&P): total assessed value (returned, depreciated) ≈ $3.974B book / ($123.9M ÷ $3.70 per $100) = **$3.349B assessed** attributed to data centers. Power capacity backing that depreciated assessed value is 862 MW turnkey + partial powered shell + partial under-construction; to remain conservative we divide by **1,000 MW** (roughly turnkey + the share of powered-shell that hosts customer-owned equipment). That yields **$3.35M C&P assessed value per MW** at mid-cycle depreciation.
  - At the **FY 2026 adopted C&P rate of $4.15 / $100** [pwc_dc2024 p.19], steady-state C&P tax is **$3.35M × 4.15 % = $138,900 / MW / year**.
- FY 2026 real-property rate (general fund + fire levy, effective FY26): **$0.920 / $100** (Stage 1 baseline; to be verified from PWC FY26 adopted budget, currently $0.9625 Board-advertised as of Apr 2025 — used here as **$0.95 / $100** placeholder; this is a **Stage 1/Stage 2 dependency** and may be revised when the Stage 6 model is integrated).
  - Real-property tax at $9.49M/MW × 0.95 % = **$90,200 / MW / year**.
- BPOL and fee-and-license revenue: from [pwc_dc2024 p.23], TY 2024 yielded $2.5M on 862 MW turnkey + 400 MW powered shell + portion of under-construction, i.e. roughly **$2,000 / MW / year** — small, but included.

**Combined steady-state county tax yield per MW (IT load):**

- Real-property: ~$90,200 / MW / yr
- C&P: ~$138,900 / MW / yr
- Fees/BPOL: ~$2,000 / MW / yr
- **Total: ~$231,000 / MW / yr (derived)**

This figure is slightly below the TY 2024 realized blended yield ($293.7M / 862 MW turnkey = $340,700 / MW) because the realized figure is inflated by (a) the new-construction supplemental assessments that accrue during build-out ($1.418B in TY 2024 alone) and (b) the transition year spike from the C&P rate change from $2.15 to $3.70. At mid-cycle stabilization those one-time components decay. We therefore adopt **$231K/MW/yr** as the Stage 6 base case and will test **$280K/MW/yr** as a high-confidence alternative.

### 4.2 Annualized tax yield foregone

At 2,000 MW IT load at stabilization:

- **Base case (derived, $231K/MW/yr): ~$462 million / year** in county general-fund tax revenue foregone at full stabilization.
- **Upper-bound case (derived, $280K/MW/yr — matches the TY 2024 realized ratio adjusted for the $4.15 rate): ~$560 million / year.**
- **Developer-stated case:** QTS testimony cited **~$500 million / year in annual tax revenue** at full buildout; Compass and their economists cited aggregate project tax payments of **~$400 million / year** [datacenterfrontier2023]. Our derived range brackets both.

These figures should be contrasted with **TY 2024 realized** total PWC data-center tax revenue of **$293.7 million** (the existing 862-MW turnkey fleet + powered shell + under-construction assets). The Digital Gateway at stabilization would have **more than doubled** that base.

### 4.3 Phasing / time-value caveat

None of those dollars would land in FY 2027 or FY 2028. The Compass first-building target was "approximately 2026" [datacenterfrontier2023] — i.e., a single building or two. Full Compass buildout was signaled over **10 years**; QTS on a comparable cadence. A reasonable phasing for Pre-Cancellation Digital Gateway in the Stage 6 integrated model is:

| Fiscal year | MW in service (IT load, Digital Gateway only) | Annual incremental tax yield (derived, $231K/MW) |
|---|---|---|
| FY27 | 50 | $11.5M |
| FY28 | 200 | $46M |
| FY29 | 450 | $104M |
| FY30 | 750 | $173M |
| FY31 | 1,050 | $243M |
| …stabilization (c. FY36) | 2,000 | $462M |

Across the **five-year forecast horizon FY27–FY31**, the cumulative county-level foregone Digital Gateway revenue in the base case is on the order of **$577 million (derived)**, before considering spillover to other operators (Stage 3) or second-order effects on real estate, BPOL, or the schools-transfer share (Stages 4-5). This is the Pre-Cancellation Digital Gateway minus CAPEX Spillover delta that the Stage 6 model must generate.
